Three ways the app could look

All three run on the real data: 10,055 Srinagar businesses and 150 sights. Type the way people talk, like "I want to buy bread", "need dawai" or "I want to visit Mughal gardens". Results come nearest first from the area you pick. There are no icons or logos, only type, colour and layout.

A · Signboard bold and local

Results look like the painted boards above Srinagar's shops. Big names, strong colours, readable at a glance in a busy bazaar.

B · Parchi quiet and fast

One question, answered like a shopkeeper's handwritten bill: numbered, with distances lined up. It loads instantly and works on slow connections.

C · Map guide for visitors

The map comes first, with lettered labels like a printed city guide. Good for tourists who think in places, not shop names.

How the search reads a request

No AI service is needed. A list of words maps everyday asks, in English, Hindi-Urdu and Kashmiri, to shop types. A few phrases decide whether someone wants to buy, eat, stay or visit. Results are then sorted by distance from the area the person picks. In the real app that would be their phone's location.

When nothing matches, the app says so and offers "Add this shop". Every failed search shows us a shop the map is missing.
